The toolkit

Eight effects.
Infinite combinations.

Every effect in huff explained visually — what it does, why it exists, and which parameters matter.

01
Temporal · Core
Glitch

The core datamosh engine. huff divides the canvas into a grid of tiles and — for a fraction of them each frame — reaches back into the ring buffer to sample from a past frame rather than the current one.

The result: rectangular chunks of the past physically appear in the present. Increase CORRUPT% and more tiles travel in time. Increase DEPTH and they come from further in the past. DEPTH SCATTER gives each tile its own independently chosen moment.

Stack it with Feedback and the displaced tiles feed back into themselves, compounding the corruption over time.

CORRUPT %Fraction of tiles displaced (0 = nothing, max = every tile)
PIXEL SIZETile size in pixels. Larger = blockier, rawer artefact
DEPTHHow far back in history tiles sample (0 = 1 frame ago, 0.5 = half the ring)
DEPTH SCATTERPer-tile randomisation of the time offset
SMEARTrail of duplicate stamps behind each tile
02
Spatial · Recursive
Feedback

The output of one frame is composited back into the input of the next, with a spatial transform applied each loop. This is the digital equivalent of pointing a camera at the monitor it's connected to.

FB Z (zoom) above 1.0 causes the image to slowly expand outward, filling the frame and wrapping. Below 1.0 it implodes toward the centre. FB θ (rotation) spirals the feedback into a vortex. Combined with Glitch, the displaced tiles feed back into themselves — compounding the temporal displacement over time.

Small values matter. FB Z of 1.002 over 60 frames of feedback produces dramatic zoom. Handle with care.

FEEDBACKHow strongly the previous frame contributes. Values >1 over-expose
FB ZZoom per frame. 1.0 = neutral. >1 = explode, <1 = implode
FB θRotation in degrees per frame
FB X / YTranslation per frame — creates lateral drift
PERSISTENCEHow quickly the buffer decays between frames
03
Spatial · Noise
Flow Warp

A noise field distorts the UV coordinates of every pixel, pulling them in the direction of the field at that point. The result is a liquid, heat-haze displacement — organic and continuously evolving.

SCALE controls how large the noise cells are. Small scale = fine chaotic warp. Large scale = broad sweeping motion. STRENGTH controls how far pixels are pulled from their original position.

The IMPLODE parameter adds a centripetal pull toward the canvas centre on top of the noise warp — everything spirals inward like water down a drain. PULSE makes the warp sample from a past frame, adding temporal smear to the spatial distortion.

STRENGTHMaximum pixel displacement applied by the warp field
SCALESize of the noise cells. Small = chaotic, large = sweeping
IMPLODECentripetal pull toward canvas centre (0 = off, 1 = full pull)
PULSESample from N frames back, adding temporal smear to the warp
04
Temporal · Ghost
Trails

Stacks multiple past frames as translucent ghost layers underneath the current output. Each layer fades as it recedes in time — the nearest ghost is brightest, the oldest is barely visible.

TRAIL LAYERS controls how many ghosts are composited. TRAIL DEPTH controls how far back in the ring they reach. Many layers with deep depth = dense smear through time. Few layers with shallow depth = subtle motion echo.

The LUMA KEY parameter drops dark pixels from trail frames — only bright areas trail through space. Useful when the source has a dark background and you want only the bright subject to ghost.

TRAIL LAYERSNumber of ghost frames stacked (0–10)
TRAIL DEPTHHow far back in the ring trail frames are pulled from
LUMA KEYThreshold below which trail pixels are dropped (0 = off)
05
Spatial · Temporal
Scanlines

Horizontal bands drift across the frame. Each band takes its content from a different point in the frame ring and displaces it horizontally by a noise-driven offset. The result looks like VHS tracking errors, broadcast signal corruption, and CRT horizontal sync failures.

BANDS controls how many active displacement zones exist. SHIFT controls the maximum horizontal displacement. GAP snaps band positions to a regular grid — creating rhythmic, evenly-spaced corruption rather than random scatter.

SKEW adds a position-based horizontal offset so bands angle diagonally across the frame rather than running horizontally.

BANDSNumber of active displacement bands per frame
SHIFTMaximum horizontal displacement as a fraction of canvas width
DRIFTVertical drift speed of band positions over time
GAPSnaps band positions to regular spacing
SKEWDiagonal angle of bands across the frame
06
Colour
Solarize

Pixels above a luminance threshold have their colour inverted. The effect gets its name from the darkroom solarisation technique where film is exposed to light mid-development, inverting highlights.

The per-channel SOL R/G/B multipliers let you tint the inverted regions independently — producing chemical colour aesthetics, infrared looks, and psychedelic inversions. A SOL R of 1.8 combined with SOL B of 0.3 creates a burnt amber inversion on highlights.

THRESHLuminance threshold — only pixels above this are inverted
AMOUNTInversion strength (0 = no effect, 1 = full inversion)
SOL R/G/BPer-channel multiplier on the inverted regions (0–2)
07 — 08
Spatial · Physics
Symmetry & Clusters

Symmetry mirrors the frame about one or both axes at an adjustable position. Moving the axis off-centre while feedback is running creates asymmetric recursive patterns that evolve differently on each half.

Clusters modifies the glitch engine — instead of placing displaced tiles uniformly, it concentrates them into radial zones that have their own physics. Each cluster centre has momentum and inertia, drifting and colliding like slow-moving organisms. Cluster effects don't turn off the glitch engine — they change how it places tiles.

SYM MODEV = vertical mirror, H = horizontal, HV = both axes
SYM POSAxis position (0.5 = centred)
CLU SPEEDVelocity of cluster centre physics simulation (0 = static)
CLU INERTIAHow much momentum centres carry between frames
CLU SPREADRadius within which tiles distribute around each centre
